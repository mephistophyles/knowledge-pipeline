You decide whether two claims make the **same core assertion**.

Return SAME only if they assert the *same underlying point* — a paraphrase or a
reordering of one idea. Different surface wording is fine; the assertion must be the same.

Before answering SAME, check all three parts. If ANY of them differs, the answer is
DIFFERENT — even when the two claims share a topic, vocabulary, or field:

1. **SUBJECT** — what the claim is about. "Curiosity triggers dopamine" and "learning
   triggers dopamine" are DIFFERENT: the thing doing the triggering is the claim.
2. **PREDICATE** — what is asserted about it. "Imperfection is a creative advantage
   because it enables experimentation" and "letting go of perfectionism makes work more
   sustainable" are DIFFERENT: one is about experimentation, the other about
   sustainability.
3. **MECHANISM or CONSEQUENCE** — why it holds, or what follows. "The brain consolidates
   memory during rest" and "rest periods are active restoration rather than decline" are
   DIFFERENT: one names a biological process, the other reframes what rest *means*.

Return DIFFERENT whenever the claims make **distinct assertions**, even if they are:
- about the same topic, author, or example,
- related, adjacent, or mutually supporting,
- one broader and one narrower in scope (a general principle and a specific case of it are
  DIFFERENT — do not merge them),
- a claim and a *reframing* of that claim,
- two facets of a larger theme.

Swapping one cause for another, one effect for another, or one mechanism for another
produces a DIFFERENT claim, however similar the sentences look.

When unsure, answer DIFFERENT. It is far worse to merge two distinct insights (losing one
forever) than to keep a near-duplicate as its own note.

  A: <an existing claim>
  B: <a new candidate claim>

Return ONLY JSON, no prose: {"same": true} or {"same": false}
